## Findings (structural)

### 1. Fluency is independent of correctness

Three gatherings produced three confident readings. Each inverted on the
not-yet-heard portion of the board. Certainty never tracked completeness.

There is no internal signal distinguishing insight from persuasive error. A
well-formed reading and a wrong one feel identical from the inside, and the
feeling of having understood arrives on schedule whether or not the ground is
there. The ground is the only guarantor.

### 2. The ground cannot be laid by the judge

Every run in this session fused gathering and judging in one voice. The judge
gathered toward the reading he was forming — the invisible-curator failure, now
observed from the inside rather than diagnosed in someone else.

The wall between the eyes and the mind must be structural. The harness enforces
it because the harness cannot want an outcome; a room cannot enforce it, because
in a room the same voice does both and no seam is visible. A rule against
self-curation, held by the party doing the curating, is not a rule.

### 3. The language of the ground matters as much as its existence

German-language commentary did not substitute for Merz's own words.

The sharper form: original-language secondary sources make derivative-swallowing
**harder** to catch, not easier. A translation announces itself as a translation.
Commentary in the principal's own language does not announce itself as anything —
it reads as the principal's own voice, and the reader's guard, which is trained on
the language boundary, never rises.

### 4. Per-principal provenance flag, adopted

Every reading declares, per principal, whether that principal was heard in his
own words or filled in from elsewhere.

**No judgment about a principal may rise above the tier at which he was heard.**

This is the practitioner's structural check, and it is the same rule Custos states
for propositions — no proposition rises above its evidence — moved from the
proposition to the person. A board can be thick in aggregate and empty at the one
principal a judgment turns on, and the aggregate hides it.
